# Customer Validation Guide

## Customer discovery

Interview people who fit the target segment. Ask about past behavior, not hypothetical enthusiasm.

Useful questions:
- Tell me about the last time this problem happened.
- What did you do?
- How often does it happen?
- What did it cost in time, money, or lost opportunity?
- What have you already tried?
- Who decides whether to buy a solution?
- What would prevent you from switching?

Avoid leading questions such as “Would you use my app?” or “Do you like this idea?”

## Evidence ladder

From weaker to stronger:
1. General positive reaction.
2. Stated interest.
3. Agreement to a follow-up.
4. Time spent testing a prototype.
5. Sharing data or integrating a workflow.
6. Signed pilot or purchase commitment.
7. Actual payment and repeated use.

This is a guide, not a universal law. Context matters.

## Experiment design

Define:
- hypothesis;
- segment;
- recruitment method;
- sample and limitations;
- offer;
- metric;
- success threshold;
- failure threshold;
- decision after results.

Never fabricate interview results or imply a small sample represents the whole market.

## Buyer and adoption analysis

Before judging demand, map:
- who feels the pain, who decides, and who pays;
- the current workaround and its switching cost;
- buying triggers and typical objections;
- where early adopters can be reached.

Separate stated interest from behavior. When no direct customer evidence exists, say so and propose interviews or experiments. Present quotes only from real sources provided or cited.
