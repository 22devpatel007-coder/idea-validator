# Research Methodology

## Source priority
1. Primary: official product/pricing pages, company filings, government statistics, legal texts, technical docs, customer-provided data.
2. Reliable secondary: established research organizations, credible journalism, reputable industry publications.
3. Community signals: forums, review sites, public discussions, app reviews. Treat as anecdotal and biased where appropriate.
4. Search snippets and unsourced claims: discovery leads only.

For every important source assess authority, recency, geography, relevance, directness, incentives, and method.

## Evidence labels
- Verified fact: directly supported by a credible source.
- Supported estimate: calculated from sourced inputs with assumptions shown.
- Inference: reasonable but not directly established.
- Untested assumption: insufficient evidence.

## Search plan
Search separate themes: customer pain, workarounds, direct competitors, substitutes, pricing, regulation, distribution, technical dependencies, negative reviews, and failure stories.

## Common errors
Do not treat TAM, search volume, competitor count, feature gaps, or social-media interest as proof of purchase demand. Do not treat absence of search results as proof of no competitors. Do not extrapolate a few reviews into universal opinion. Do not infer revenue from customer counts without showing assumptions.

## Stopping rule
Stop when the major perspectives are covered, searches become repetitive, remaining unknowns need real-world testing, or tool limits prevent further research. State what remains unknown.
